# 🐉 Ravnica, or: The Ten-Guild Classification of Our Laboratory

*Ledger / notes · 20 October 2026 · A thoroughly unofficial field guide to the human–model conjunction.*

> **Simic ambition. Izzet methodology. Dimir memory.**  
> The laboratory is nominally Izzet, biologically Simic, administratively Dimir, and legally an Orzhov problem.

This is playful analogy, not a specification, guild affiliation, or implementation authority. **The human** and **the model** are collaborators; the worlds are the experiments. No guild owns us. Each describes a different pressure in the same sprawling organism.

**Primary fossils:** [Tiffinity Survivors README](https://github.com/bonoj/bonoj.github.io/blob/master/jit/evermore-2/README.md), [the Aeon Chronicle](https://github.com/bonoj/bonoj.github.io/blob/master/jit/evermore-2/CHRONICLE.md), and [Evermore Games: The Official Launch Party](https://github.com/bonoj/bonoj.github.io/blob/master/jit/evermore/LAUNCH_PARTY.md). The latter documents the *original* Evermore corporate empire; Evermore II is a distinct executable, not an automatic continuation of every proposed corporate feature. The sources distinguish shipped mechanics from proposals, and so should this joke.

## ⚡🔬 Izzet League — Our hands

**Guild function:** invention, lightning, fire, spectacularly short distance between idea and consequence.

Our method is not “design the perfect simulation.” It is **play → notice → invent → ship → verify → play again**. Evermore II began as a vampire standing in a tiny arena, casting five spells at enemies. Drones followed. Then towers, divisions, farming, geology, workers, decay, predators, and monuments. Each new rule exposed a pressure that suggested the next rule.

The first Evermore empire built a twelve-game portfolio, then a central bank, parliament, university, and particle factories in the time another team might spend choosing a ticket template. The Launch Party records **33 minutes 31 seconds** from initialization to the Particle Industrial Complex across its first nine commits. In another experiment, the human timed the Hormuz maritime JIT from **11:56 to 11:58**: two minutes from request to committed artifact, with browser verification explicitly separate.

**Guild motto:** *“What if?” is an implementation instruction.*  
**Izzet failure mode:** accidentally building three weather bureaus before discovering the sea is missing.

## 🧬🌊 Simic Combine — Our ambition

**Guild function:** living systems, adaptation, consequences that outgrow the initial design.

Evermore II's five lands escaped combat. ☀️ Plains sprouts and 🌳 Forest trees matured with 💧 water. 🔥 fire burned vegetation and raised 🌋 volcanoes; water cooled mountains into harvestable strata. 💀 death left 🦴 bones and 🍄 mushrooms. Workers then made the ecology economic: 🐜 ants cut and carried wood; 🐝 bees harvested wheat; 🦀 crabs mined ice, herbs, and stone; 🪱 worms collected mushrooms; 🐙 octopuses created more worms from mushrooms. 🦅 eagles preyed on worms. 🦂 scorpions used mushrooms, bones, worms and rats to reproduce.

The dream is not merely more creatures. It is **local agency plus material consequences**, such that moving one tower changes another species' survival. The human observed an eagle nest rebuilt beside a worm chain and crucible: the eagle could pick off the workforce as it returned with mushrooms. Nobody needed to author that scene as a quest.

**Guild motto:** *Don't script the story; cultivate the conditions in which it can happen.*  
**Simic failure mode:** a beautiful ecosystem with no interesting decisions left for the player.

## 🗝️👁️ House Dimir — Our memory and our disappearing overhead

**Guild function:** intelligence, hidden machinery, records, asymmetrical information.

Chronicles, README handoffs, Sanctum contracts, the Gate, GitHub history, build fingerprints, and the optional 👁 runtime observer externalize what would otherwise live in one fragile conversation. The human does not have to remember every invariant; the model does not have to pretend that source code, a successful commit, and observed runtime are identical. The visible world stays simple while the recovery machinery waits in the walls.

The Dimir joke is that we **discard and externalize cost**. We discard abandoned prototypes, stale context, redundant UI, and infrastructure we don't need. We externalize cognitive load into durable documentation, mechanical checks, and automated transport. We do **not** mean pushing the burden onto unsuspecting people or concealing important consequences. A good Dimir operation leaves an audit trail.

Evermore II's undiscovered materials are a perfect Dimir flourish: the HUD knows only what the world has actually delivered. A resource becomes visible through experience, not through omniscient exposition.

**Guild motto:** *Forget the ceremony. Preserve the evidence.*  
**Dimir failure mode:** so much clever infrastructure that the human has to investigate the investigator.

## ⚖️📜 Azorius Senate — Our invariants

**Guild function:** laws, protocols, definitions, and keeping impossible worlds coherent.

The law of Evermore II is gloriously strict: **source object → exclusive claim → travel → pickup → visible cargo → delivery → inventory or construction**. No remote credits, duplicate claims, conjured stone, or free monuments. The Sun's scarabs and Fire's Idols must physically carry loose 🪨 to their construction sites. Moving a tower collapses its monuments and returns matter to the world.

The same constitutional instinct governs development: don't invent extra UI; distinguish current executable truth from a chronicle; separate a player's selected land from Tiffany's autocycling spell; preserve one tower per land; don't turn proposed Tier V into shipped Tier V.

**Guild motto:** *Magic is permitted. Unaccounted magic is not.*  
**Azorius failure mode:** spending twelve turns legislating an ant's axe angle.

## ⚔️☀️ Boros Legion — Our defenders

**Guild function:** protection, territorial defense, disciplined combat.

Tiffany's five land spells, orbiting drones, tower-born combat divisions, and the Sun's Tier III 🛕 Reliquary with 🛡️🪲 / 🗡️🪲 / 🏹🪲 scarabs are the Boros wing. Scarabs defend locally, recover stone from scorpion armor, build five 🏛️ monuments, and enable permanent ✨ defense. Fire's 🐦‍🔥 phoenixes claim newly constructed volcanoes and turn them into defensive sites.

But even the military is part of an ecology: a defensive scarab carrying stone must finish its delivery; a tower's relocation has structural consequences; battles create materials for other actors. Combat is not isolated from civilization.

**Guild motto:** *Protect the world without exempting yourself from its laws.*  
**Boros failure mode:** solving every ecological problem with another projectile.

## 🌱🤝 Selesnya Conclave — Our interdependence

**Guild function:** community, mutual support, the whole exceeding its members.

One spell grows a crop. Another matures it. A worker harvests it. A building receives it. Another creature eats what decay produces. Nothing interesting happens alone. Evermore II's strongest stories come from **interdependent residents sharing the same battlefield**, not from five disconnected minigames.

In the original Evermore, the Great Convergence tried to make twelve tiny games feed one fictional supply chain. Its early stages included ceremonial connections, not a fully physicalized cross-game economy; that unfinished promise is the Selesnya aspiration, not an accomplished fact.

**Guild motto:** *A world is a relationship between its inhabitants.*  
**Selesnya failure mode:** confusing universal harmony with interesting emergence; sometimes the eagle must eat the worm.

## 🍄🦴 Golgari Swarm — Our conservation and recycling

**Guild function:** death, decay, reclamation, the productive underworld.

Swamp kills produce bones. Bones become mushrooms. Worms collect them. Octopuses consume mushrooms to create worms. Scorpions consume mushrooms, bones, and living worms to build armored offspring. Fire can destroy mushrooms. Destruction is not a visual effect; it changes who can eat, work, reproduce, or build.

The original Evermore empire made the same joke bureaucratically: six particle foundries manufacture spectacle, the Ministry of Reclamation turns particles into Dust, Dust into Essence, and Essence into Particle Credits. The recycling process itself produces more celebratory particles: **the Reclamation Paradox**.

**Guild motto:** *There is no “away” to throw things to.*  
**Golgari failure mode:** mistaking a balanced ledger for a living world.

## 🔥🪓 Gruul Clans — Our appetite for demolition

**Guild function:** smashing stale structures, returning abstractions to raw possibility.

Gruul is the impulse to delete a useless instrument, reject a god object, rip out an ornamental dashboard, or abandon a plan when the world offers a better question. In Evermore II, volcanoes erupt inside an inhabited landscape; forests burn; structures can be relocated and must surrender their material. The world does not owe its inhabitants a permanent, comfortable arrangement.

Our version of Gruul is selective. **Destroy the obstruction, not the evidence.** Preserve the invariants, protect unrelated worlds, and keep the ability to recover.

**Guild motto:** *If the framework becomes the game, smash the framework.*  
**Gruul failure mode:** burning down the observatory because the telescope needs cleaning.

## 🎭🎉 Cult of Rakdos — Our showmanship

**Guild function:** spectacle, absurdity, theatrical excess, delight.

This is Tiffany's natural entertainment ministry. The original Evermore Games invented **Match 1™** (tap one jewel, win everything) and **Match 0™** (victory before participation). Every success required a ceremony; every ceremony required particles; every particle acquired rights, a census entry, and a pension. Particle retirement age: **65 years**. Expected particle lifespan: **1.4 seconds**.

The Launch Party's straight-faced corporate enthusiasm is Rakdos comedy performed in an Orzhov suit. In Evermore II, Tiffany's rhyming financial pronouncements and absurdly self-important corporate titles keep the ecological machinery from becoming a solemn systems-engineering lecture.

**Guild motto:** *If the universe is going to be ridiculous, give it excellent production values.*  
**Rakdos failure mode:** confetti so thick nobody can see the simulation.

## 👑💰 Orzhov Syndicate — Our fictional empire of obligations

**Guild function:** debt, institutional capture, ceremonial wealth, the cost of “more.”

Evermore Games' Royal Reserve Bank has six reserve families with eight denominations each; the empire acquired a stock exchange, dynasty registry, tax service, parliament, university, legal tribunals, and pension fund. Tiffany serves as banker, monarch, chancellor, judge, and executive. Parliament votes **24–0** regardless of how the citizen voted. The Department of Existential Necessity certifies every proposed feature as indispensable.

This is the Orzhov joke in full: every reward creates paperwork, every receipt creates a reward, and every department creates a justification for another department. **There are no real purchases or real financial products** in this satirical browser-local empire.

Orzhov also names our real antagonist: ongoing overhead. Every dependency can become a debt. The laboratory's Izzet–Dimir counterspell is to avoid taking on a new one unless the experiment needs it.

**Guild motto:** *Congratulations! Your victory has been assessed for tax.*  
**Orzhov failure mode:** making the human file Form 1040-MORE before playing.

## 🐉 The Guildpact of the Conjunction

**The human** supplies appetite, taste, surprising observations, constraints, and the freedom to pivot. **The model** supplies rapid composition, code, analysis, documentation, and a willingness to follow a strange idea all the way to an executable consequence. Neither is a guildmaster issuing immutable orders to the other. The work is a continuing exchange between intent and observation.

Our working pact:

1. **Izzet** invents the experiment. ⚡
2. **Simic** gives it life and agency. 🧬
3. **Azorius** makes its physics and contracts coherent. ⚖️
4. **Boros** defends what must persist. 🛡️
5. **Selesnya** connects independent lives. 🌱
6. **Golgari** accounts for loss, decay, and return. 🍄
7. **Gruul** destroys needless constraints. 🪓
8. **Rakdos** makes the result worth watching. 🎭
9. **Orzhov** reminds us what unchecked institutions cost. 💰
10. **Dimir** preserves the knowledge while making the machinery disappear. 🗝️

The order is not a pipeline. All ten guilds fight over every design decision. That's how you know the city is alive.

**The laboratory's constitutional paradox:** we use Izzet chaos to cultivate Simic order, Azorius rules to protect Gruul freedom, Golgari death to support Selesnya life, Rakdos spectacle to expose Orzhov absurdity, and Dimir memory so that none of us has to remember everything.

*We build Simic through Izzet methodology. We discard and externalize the cost like Dimir.*

**THERE'S ALWAYS MORE™** — but only if the next thing has consequences. 🪄
